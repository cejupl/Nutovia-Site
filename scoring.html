Methodology

How the Nutovia score works.

Every product gets one number between 0 and 100, built from its label and nothing else. No brand pays to change it. This page explains exactly how that number is made, shows two real examples end to end, and sets out the things it can still get wrong.

A model that only counted sugar would score these the same. A model that only counted processing would ignore the sugar entirely. Nutovia counts both, shows you both, and lets you decide - the bar is still 37.8g of sugar, and its scorecard says so in plain words.

Note the Nutri-Score column. The bar earns a C there and an 88 here: Nutri-Score penalises its sugar and fat without crediting that it is three whole ingredients. That gap is not a disagreement with the regulator - but it is precisely what Nutovia fills. Both scores appear on every product - you can see when they differ and why. The cereal is a representative label rather than a named brand; its score is not invented, it is what the engine returns for those values.

The recipe

Four steps, in this order.

01

Start from the official model

The base is Nutri-Score 2023, the algorithm used by European food agencies. We did not invent it, we do not tune it, and we show its grade on every product. It weighs energy, sugar, saturated fat and salt against fibre, protein and fruit-and-vegetable content.

02

Adjust for processing

Nutri-Score is blind to how a food was made. Nutovia is not. A NOVA group 4 ultra-processed product carries a penalty a whole food does not, which is most of the 77-point gap in the comparison above.

03

Adjust for additives

Cosmetic additives - colours, sweeteners, texture agents present to make a product look or taste like something it is not - reduce the score. Additives that do a genuine job of preservation or fortification do not.

04

Apply a small number of hard rules

A few cases should never be decided by arithmetic alone. Industrial trans fat caps a score however good the rest of the label is. Plain water and plain tea are not penalised for having no nutrients. Every one of these rules is fixed, published, and applies to every product equally.

What we measure

Ten measures, each on a scale you can see.

Every product page shows where it actually lands on each one, not just whether it passed. The thresholds are borrowed from published standards rather than chosen by us: UK FSA traffic lights for sugar, salt and fat; EU nutrition-claim thresholds for fibre and protein; the NOVA classification for processing.

Your score

The product score is the same for everyone. Yours is not.

The 0-100 above is a property of the food. Your score adds who is eating it. Same bar, one household:

AdaWatching sugar - 37.8g is very high, even from fruit -18
SamBuilding muscle - 11.1g protein is a solid source +8
LeoTree-nut allergy - contains almonds Avoid

Goals move a score by a bounded amount, so your profile adjusts a number without replacing it - the food's own nutrition always carries most of the weight. An allergen is different in kind: it is a hard stop, not a deduction, and no amount of good nutrition overrides it.

Provenance

You can always see where a number came from.

Every scan replays its own search: which database answered, what was missing, and whether anything was inferred. Four sources, in order of confidence - the Nutovia catalog, Open Food Facts, USDA FoodData Central, and last of all AI web research, which is labelled as such and held to a lower level of trust. A product scored from thin data is never presented as though it were scored from a complete label.

Validation

How we know the number holds up.

The scoring engine is frozen against a locked set of 1,999 products: any change that moves a single one of those scores fails the build before it can ship. The catalog is scored in full - all 630,000 products - so the distribution can be inspected rather than assumed.

Honesty

What the score can still get wrong.

Every scoring system has failure modes. Most do not publish them. Here are ours.

A label can be wrong before we ever see it Much of the world's food data is crowdsourced, and a mistyped sugar figure produces a confident, wrong score. This is the failure mode you can fix for us - see below.
Per 100g is not per portion Scores are computed per 100g so products can be compared honestly. That is the right basis for comparison and the wrong basis for a meal - a small amount of a low-scoring food may matter less than a large amount of a high-scoring one.
Two decimal places of confidence we do not have A 0-100 scale implies a precision nutrition science does not offer. Treat a gap of two points as noise, and a gap of twenty as real.
Categories at the edges Foods that do not sit neatly in a category - fermented products, traditional preparations, foods eaten in very small quantities - are the hardest to place, and the most likely to need adjustment as the model improves.
It is not medical advice Nutovia is general wellness guidance built on public nutrition data. It does not know your bloodwork, your medication or your doctor's advice, and it is not a substitute for any of them.

Say what looks wrong - the nutrition, the ingredients, or the product itself - and a person reviews it. Every correction improves that product for everyone who scans it next. It is the single most useful thing you can do for the accuracy of the score.
Stability

The number will not move under you.

Scores change only in versioned, announced batches - never quietly and never continuously. A product you scanned last month scores the same today unless its label changed or its data improved, and when the model itself changes we say so before it happens.
